# Animation scaffold

## Now (2026-10-14)

Goal: the film is scaffolded by a timeline and tracks that code reads, an animatic the owner watches, and gates that are probe scripts with positive controls, so the same chain and the same gates serve the next animation project unchanged.
Slice: Tier 1 (code primitives) and Tier 2 (probes + gates) in one worker pass each, then Tier 3 (the `animation_animatic` chain, created canonically) run once end to end on the 65 s ledger as the trail.

## Rulings

- **D80** (owner 2026-10-14): a new per-episode chain, not an extension of the per-shot chain. The animatic sits between pre-production and `showcase_shot`.
- **D81** (owner 2026-10-14, "heavily consider the gates"): every gate on the chain is a probe script that reads contracts and frames, exits non-zero on a failure it names in reader voice, and ships with a positive control that must trip. A gate with no control is advisory and says so in its description.
- **D82** (planner default, flips if the owner objects): the chain, its steps and its gates are project-agnostic resources under a new `animation` prompt category, parameterized by `episode_root` and `renderer`; the showcase is their first episode. The rendering-side primitives live in `src/library/animation/` with an enforced import boundary, so they can be lifted into a template or package later without a rewrite.
- **D83** (owner 2026-10-14, "define the poses at the keyframes for the animation's length"): pose-first. Every shot's keys are written as a pose sheet before any plate exists: a line of action the solver can build (class, bow, side), weight on one foot, one side leading, a silhouette kind, a plate family; every breakdown is a pose (passing position, anticipation, overshoot, settle), never a favour alone; secondaries lag; holds move. The inbetweens are verified against the twos standard by probes (C31–C33), so the 2000s Flash look (straight chords, even spacing, everything on one frame, frozen holds) is refused by a gate, not by eye.
- **D84** (planner default 2026-10-14, flips if the owner objects): the pose step is a new step `animation_poses` in the per-shot chain between the timing chart and the key sheet, with gate `animation-pose-contract`; the render step gains `animation-inbetween-probes`. Plates are cut per plate family the pose sheet names, so Tier 5.1 follows Tier 6.
- **D85** (owner 2026-10-15, "we should be building and pushing our assets and joints here rather than delegating the design work to the generator"): key plates and their joints are built in the project from the pose sheet's solved joints through the plate family's mesh; an image generator only proposes a NEW silhouette family the rig cannot hold, which is then cut into its own mesh and registered by a toolchain, never by eye. Every asset is pushed toward the stated baseline (creative-spec v4-brief-18, the model sheet, rubric C20–C22, C31) and measured on the qualities the prompts contract; the key gate reads those measurements.
- **D86** (owner 2026-10-15, "only the latest chains are present … hold and wait until the entire demo and release is ready instead of only committing and merging progress"): the showcase server catalogue keeps only the current chains (`showcase_preproduction`, `animation_animatic`, `showcase_shot`, `showcase_asset_muse`) and the prompts they call; superseded chains are deleted through the client with receipts. The work is committed as checkpoints on `docs/creative-showcase` and held in one draft PR (#494, label `blocked`) that merges only when the demo and the release are ready. The interview-boundary commits that shared the branch were parked on `feat/interview-boundary`. Third-party reference media, renders, the v2 pass and the generator's rejects are ignored, never committed.
- **D87** (owner 2026-10-16): rendering is made faster by hashing and caching, not by changing the look: every layer's inputs (the contracts, the plates, the world contract, the camera) are hashed, a distinct drawing is rendered once per layer and frames are composed from cached layers with the camera as a 2D transform on ones, worlds bake per beat, and a layer whose hash is unchanged is not re-rendered. The browser stays as the final compositor and the visual confirmation; it is never skipped for the frames the owner watches. Shading models (BRDF, path tracing) are skipped: the film's laws are flat roles and light as a volume. The render prompts and gates read the render manifest, so a stale layer is refused by hash.
- Metrics triage (2026-10-14): plate-conformance proxies (contour width, fill class, hatch direction, bloom width, luminance peaks, head counts, scale drift) run once at sheet acceptance and never on the loop. Loop gates are the episode-tier probes in Tier 2.

## The six tracks on one timeline

| Track    | Carries                                                             | Source today                                              | Quantised                |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| pacing   | beat frames, holds, cut / no-cut, crossing windows                  | `v3/beats-v2.json`, CalArts hold band (`craft-ref-01.md`) | frames                   |
| blocking | actor and prop positions and scale, gates, emitter anchor, per beat | `v3/layouts/*.json`, `STAGE` in `worlds/contract.ts`      | per beat, eased on twos  |
| camera   | centre, zoom, rotation; the move's leading line                     | `v3/layouts/*.json`, board §8 cards                       | ones                     |
| keys     | named poses with holds and breakdowns                               | `showcase_key_sheet`, `liftChart`                         | twos                     |
| light    | emitter path and intensity, radiance, hue pair                      | `shots.json` `hue`, D74 colour script                     | ones                     |
| look     | world per beat, crossing operator, texture via the world contract   | `v3/worlds/*.json`, `Crossing.tsx`                        | per beat; reduce on twos |

Editing rhythm is the pacing track's data. Texture is the look track through the world contracts. The exposure sheet is the per-frame projection of all six.
