## Goals

### 4. DISTINCT DIRECTIONS

All three are original constructions, with roomy hanging torso mass and controllable forearms. None uses a sash, cultural emblem, adventurer hardware or a skin-tight substitute for drape.

- **A — offset-panel pullover; recommended first test.** Rounded neckline; defined shoulder/armhole; straight, narrower forearm sleeve ending above the hand; one short overlapping diagonal hem panel anchored at the opposite side. Loose cropped/tapered trousers keep the upper construction visible. Hair is one broad uneven swept crest with a few escaped angular tufts and an open forehead. Benefit: one deliberate cut reads ordinary at rest and reveals overlap/tension on reach. Risk: the diagonal panel becomes a painted shadow rather than real overlap. Mind Game's simple regions inform mass economy; the balloon-top control bounds the sleeve.
- **B — long collarless raglan shirt.** Raglan shoulder line; roomy torso falls lower; split side vents and a straight narrow cuff permit arm travel. Trousers are simpler and narrower beneath the longer upper mass. Hair is a low backward fan with unequal curved clumps, not the same ring-like top loop. Benefit: a different long hanging silhouette. Risk: another oversized sleeve or apparent robe; length alone does not pass. Loose-drape intent remains, with a changed arm/hem construction.
- **C — short plain outer layer over narrow sleeves.** A softly structured, collarless sleeveless outer top with a displaced simple front overlap sits above a narrow-sleeve inner layer; trousers carry more rounded lower volume. Hair has a compact uneven crown/gathered mass and one loose trailing lock. Benefit: layering makes arm/torso separation explicit. Risk: utility-vest or decorative costume reading; no pockets, belts, badges or added gear. Its two-layer construction is the strongest alternative if A cannot show a real hem overlap.

## Execution

### 5. PAIRWISE CRITIQUE

A versus B asks whether a short local overlap or long hanging shirt best carries sophisticated ordinary drape. A versus C asks whether one deliberately cut layer is sufficient or two separate layers make contact construction clearer. Choose A provisionally for economy; reverse to C if its overlap remains a shadow decoration or the reaching forearm merges into the chest. These are shape proposals, not source-derived numeric ratios.

### 6. CONTROLLED VISUAL EVIDENCE / NEXT ASSET STEPS

1. Make one comparison of A/B/C full-body cuts and hair masses with the same compact face/body, camera, rest pose, key/fill, backdrop and low-chroma cloth value family. Include a small same-action reach/contact inset per candidate if feasible. These are held intentions, not deterministic generation guarantees.
2. Root compares garment occupancy, shoulder/armhole placement, sleeve opening, torso/hem overlaps and hair negative spaces before adding texture. A repeated balloon top under different hair fails the comparison. No candidate is locked merely because a sheet exists.
3. For the chosen cut, next prepare **front and three-quarter rest, reach and visible-contact keys**. Keep light/camera/face consistent; expose how cloth joins travel with the body. This is the smallest construction proof before motion. C4's single sheet may not establish all these views.
4. Add a small integrated trio/value strip with original Codex/Clawd after cut selection. Only then feed the accepted construction into the shared color/story and technique passes. Encode reusable MCP review standards after proof; do not install a gate from this untested proposal.

Palette collision proposal: reserve recognizable blue for Codex and coral for Clawd. Try lighter low-chroma warm-neutral human cloth, with a limited slate/charcoal underside and hair mass. Keep the human head/hands readable beside both companions and avoid dark hair merging with Codex's screen. Pigment changes must be tested in an actual trio and grayscale/value view; no full palette or RGB ladder is locked here.

## Evaluation

### 7. SYNTHESIS / FAILURE CHECKS

**Recommended:** A changes the armhole, forearm and real hem overlap while keeping generous torso drape. **Nearest alternative:** C trades simplicity for explicit layering. **Weakest point:** a plausible still may hide an impossible reach/contact arrangement. **Counter-vector:** narrowing every garment into basic fitted clothes would lose the desired ordinary sophistication.

- **Edges:** silhouette, occlusion/contact and selected fold marks have different jobs. No uniform outline, dark stroke on every light-facing edge or decorative hatching conceals a wrong join.
- **Folds:** tension connects armhole/elbow/side anchor; compression accumulates where cloth meets itself. A diagonal painted stripe, unchanged folds across all poses or four-corner plane shear is insufficient.
- **Overlap:** shoulder/elbow/cuff remain separable from chest; front/back layers and hand/contact ordering are visible. An inflated chest sleeve, doubled arm, detached cuff or fixed open palm falsely “grasping” fails.
- **Hair:** unequal masses and gaps retain forehead/eyes and face identity. Changing only hair while repeating the rejected cut fails. No photoreal strand/fur treatment is required.
- **Cast/value:** human clothes do not compete with Clawd's coral or swallow Codex's dark screen. Do not solve this by recoloring the mascots or inventing species.

These are future visual checks, not automatic art scores, observed moving proof or measured perceptibility thresholds.
